\chapter{ALD HfO$_2$ Deposition}
\label{chap:ald}

\section{Starting Conditions and Predeposition Preparation}
\subsection{Starting Film, Substrate, and Device Conditions}

Starting conditions are bounded by the reported HZO composition, deposited thickness, wafer temperature, and capacitor diameter, so comparisons remain configuration-specific rather than sharing a universal baseline \cite{kim2020tin,kim2014inductive}. One study defines a single equimolar Hf:Zr deposition condition and device-size range, while another contributes only a thicker-film condition \cite{kim2020tin,kim2014inductive}. The five-row evidence base spans just these two sources; it does not support broader claims about substrate material, device architecture, or alternative starting films \cite{kim2020tin,kim2014inductive}.
\subsection{Surface Cleaning and Exposure Control}

Reported surface preparation is not a single interchangeable clean: one substrate flow uses SPM followed by RCA SC-1/SC-2, while another uses sequential ultrasonic acetone, ethanol, and DI-water steps \cite{park2022chamber,park2023seed}. Vacuum-break exposure is separately associated with increased carbon and tetragonal-phase stabilization, making exposure history part of the starting condition \cite{schroeder2023road}. Other documented wet-chemical steps occur after electrode formation and therefore cannot define a common predeposition protocol \cite{choi2025purge,kim2020tin,neuber2022peald}.
\subsection{Interface and Seed-Layer Engineering}

Interface preparation is an explicit design variable: reported interlayers are introduced to alter crystallization, limit interlayer formation or defect migration, interrupt HZO grain-boundary paths, and buffer oxygen exchange \cite{schroeder2023road,liao2023postmoore}. Al$_2$O$_3$ can serve as an internal dielectric or diffusion barrier, but the reported tradeoff includes voltage division and an added depolarization field; Pt insertion instead targets metal/oxide interfacial-layer suppression and a higher interface barrier \cite{schroeder2023road,liao2023postmoore,kashir2021wake}. The seed-layer study retains top, bottom, dual, and unseeded configurations as distinct specimens rather than pooling them, with the dual ZrO$_2$ layers directly resolved near the interfaces \cite{park2023seed}. Likewise, HfO$_2$/ZrO$_2$ superlattices remain sensitive to the starting layer and sublayer thickness, while a Zr-rich middle layer defines a separate trilayer architecture \cite{schroeder2023road,liu2025zrrich}. For Si-doped stacks, the slightly thicker PEALD SiO$_2$ monolayer relative to thermal ALD shifts the doping concentration, so nominal cycle ratios do not guarantee equivalent composition \cite{neuber2022peald}.

\section{ALD Film-Formation Sequence}
\subsection{Hf-Precursor Delivery and First Half-Cycle}

Precursor delivery is represented as a set of process-specific first half-cycles, not as one transferable Hf dose \cite{song2021ferro,choi2025purge,kaatranen2026unif}. The supported routes span cyclopentadienyl, alkylamide, TEMA-, and TDMA-based precursors; the alkylamide routes are reported for thermal and plasma-enhanced ALD with limited thermal stability, while organometallic Hf precursors bearing organic ligands are associated with residual impurities \cite{song2021ferro,schroeder2023road}. Process-specific delivery detail is concentrated in the TEMA-based PEALD study, where source and manifold conditioning, Ar-assisted dosing at fixed chamber pressure, and unequal Hf and Zr pulse lengths form one coupled recipe \cite{choi2025purge}. Those delivery values therefore remain attached to that reactor and chemistry rather than serving as defaults for the other precursor families \cite{choi2025purge}. The remaining temperature observations are likewise conditional: thermal and plasma-enhanced studies pair their substrate settings with particular reactors, films, and precursor systems \cite{choi2025purge,park2023seed,kim2014inductive,neuber2022peald,kaatranen2026unif,liu2025zrrich,kashir2021wake}. Accordingly, precursor identity, delivery conditioning, reactor mode, and the surrounding film specification are treated as one first-half-cycle definition \cite{song2021ferro,choi2025purge}.
\subsection{Purge and Co-Reactant Half-Cycle}

After the metal dose, first-purge evidence is confined to one HZO PEALD study \cite{choi2025purge}. That flow uses Ar at the same reported chamber pressure as precursor dosing and varies the precursor-purge duration as an experimental series; it does not establish a purge recipe for the other reactor or precursor variants \cite{choi2025purge}. Co-reactant coverage is broader but remains route-specific \cite{song2021ferro,schroeder2023road}. Water is reported in thermal ALD flows, ozone appears at study-specific concentrations and pulse lengths, and plasma-enhanced routes use oxygen-based exposures \cite{kim2020tin,kim2014inductive,neuber2022peald,kaatranen2026unif,choi2025purge,park2023seed,liu2025zrrich,kashir2021wake}. In one PEALD sequence, the Hf and Zr oxygen-plasma steps receive different power--duration pairs, whereas the silicon-doping PEALD flow couples oxygen with a capacitively coupled Ar plasma \cite{choi2025purge,neuber2022peald}. The oxidant step therefore remains paired with the relevant metal or dopant subcycle \cite{choi2025purge,neuber2022peald}. Second-purge coverage is limited to one entry identifying nitrogen as purge and carrier gas for thermal HZO ALD; no supported second-purge duration, pressure, or broader mechanism is available \cite{kim2014inductive}.
\subsection{Cycle Architecture and Repetition}

Cycle repetition is configured around the intended composition and thickness rather than one fixed template \cite{park2022chamber,choi2025purge,park2023seed,kaatranen2026unif}. In one thermal HZO series, the supercycle count changes with chamber temperature while the deposited thickness remains 10~nm, so the count is not temperature-independent \cite{park2022chamber}. A PEALD route instead uses a defined Hf:Zr dose ratio and supercycle total to approach the same thickness \cite{choi2025purge}. Another nanolaminate series holds the combined HfO$_2$ and ZrO$_2$ count at 120 cycles while changing the block length, separating sequence architecture from total cycle count \cite{park2023seed}. The SS/SL comparison similarly reaches a common thickness target through alternating single cycles or grouped symmetric blocks \cite{kaatranen2026unif}. Discrete-feeding ALD adds another conditional architecture by repeating the precursor half-cycle before the reactant half-cycle \cite{schroeder2023road}. Repetition count, block size, half-cycle duplication, and deposition condition therefore remain jointly specified \cite{schroeder2023road,park2022chamber,park2023seed}.

\section{Post-Deposition Processing}
\subsection{Post-Deposition Thermal Treatment}

Post-deposition treatment is defined by the state of the stack at heating, not by an anneal label alone. PDA denotes uncapped RTA, whereas PMA follows TiN capping; elsewhere, PDA precedes upper-electrode deposition and PMA follows the top electrode \cite{song2021ferro,park2022chamber,park2023seed}. The anneal sequence and electrode boundary condition are therefore coupled \cite{song2021ferro,park2023seed}. A direct capped--uncapped comparison likewise places RTP after Ni formation for the capped MIM structure and before top-electrode deposition for the uncapped sample, so the two cases cannot share one recipe description \cite{kim2014inductive}.

Reported sequences impose still narrower placement constraints. One flow anneals after TiN top-electrode patterning but before bottom-electrode localization to minimize interface damage, whereas another performs RTA after bottom-TiN deposition and before HZO deposition specifically to isolate stress from the top electrode \cite{kaatranen2026unif,kim2020tin}. Nitrogen RTA series, hydrogen-containing furnace treatment, high-pressure oxygen annealing, and NH$_3$ plasma pretreatment belong to different configurations or stated objectives; ambient therefore cannot be detached from sequence and purpose \cite{park2022chamber,kim2020tin,liao2023postmoore}. The BEOL-constrained route additionally requires the entire process, including thermal treatment, to remain below 400~$^\circ$C, making higher-temperature crystallization flows inapplicable to that stated constraint \cite{schroeder2023road,neuber2022peald}.

\subsection{Electrode and Stack Integration}

Electrode integration is an ordered stack-construction problem because a nominal conductor does not specify its position, deposition history, or interface exposure \cite{schroeder2023road,neuber2022peald}. The SNAP TiN/HZO/TiN route forms the electrodes and oxide in one ALD tool without a vacuum break, while another TiN stack uses ALD for the bottom electrode and PVD for the top electrode; these routes impose different interface histories \cite{schroeder2023road,neuber2022peald}.

Position-specific processing also changes downstream boundary conditions \cite{schroeder2023road,kim2020tin}. For TiN, bottom-electrode oxidation promotes the ferroelectric phase through oxygen scavenging, while top-electrode microtexture depends on deposition pressure; a room-temperature top layer suppresses monoclinic-phase formation through stress-induced crystallization, whereas high-temperature top-electrode deposition is a reported limitation \cite{schroeder2023road,kim2020tin}. Top and bottom TiN designations are therefore not interchangeable \cite{kim2020tin}. In the W-based purge study, the bottom electrode, top capping layer, and Pt top contact are separate operations, with the Pt contact introduced for electrical characterization rather than serving as the W cap \cite{choi2025purge}. Likewise, Au/Pd functions as a hard mask, and Ti/Au forms contacts to TiN electrodes; neither description defines the capacitor electrode stack by itself \cite{kim2020tin,kaatranen2026unif}. Material substitutions retain their own conditions: RuO$_2$ and IrO$_2$ supply oxygen and improve endurance relative to TaN and TiN, whereas W is tied to its thermal-expansion-driven tensile-strain behavior \cite{schroeder2023road}. The hybrid W/Pt/HZO/W configuration explicitly assigns top W and bottom Pt to combine their advantages, so collapsing it to a generic W/Pt designation loses the reported stack orientation \cite{kashir2021wake}.

\section{Materials, Reactor Platforms, and Equipment}

\section{Critical Process and Device Parameters}
\subsection{Film Thickness and Growth per Cycle}

Cycle-count interpretation must retain chemistry and temperature: independent HfO$_2$/ZrO$_2$ calibrations use TEMA-Hf/TEMA-Zr in PEALD or TEMAH/TEMAZ with O$_3$ at $250\,^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$, and HZO growth per supercycle decreases as chamber temperature rises from 120 to $250\,^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ \cite{choi2025purge,park2022chamber,park2023seed,kashir2021wake}. Consequently, the ellipsometric thickness measured on Si reference pieces and the ALD-HZO thickness series describe different specimens rather than a shared thickness window \cite{kim2014inductive,kaatranen2026unif}. For Si-containing stacks, the standard thermal-ALD SiO$_2$ doping-layer process deposits significantly more slowly than PEALD, so the rate comparison remains specific to thermal-versus-plasma SiO$_2$ layer formation \cite{neuber2022peald}.
\subsection{Conformality and Uniformity}

Conformality evidence applies to a 10 nm ferroelectric-HfO$_2$ MFM capacitor in a high-aspect-ratio 3D 1T--1C geometry; the step-coverage claim is comparative to PVD/CVD, so it does not establish blanket wafer uniformity \cite{song2021ferro}. For standard thermal and hybrid plasma-enhanced Si:HfO$_2$ processes, thickness differs across cycling ratios even though both show similar thickness standard deviations, while the absence of crossflow patterns is reported only for hybrid layers \cite{neuber2022peald}. Wafer-scale interpretation depends on sampling 140 sites of $1\times1$ cm$^2$ and separating center-area from full-wafer results at $\pm3.0$ V \cite{kaatranen2026unif}. A precursor-inlet polarization minimum coincides with thicker HZO, whereas the single failed SL site is attributed to incomplete Ti/Au removal rather than the film \cite{kaatranen2026unif}.
\subsection{Polarization and Coercive Field}

Polarization and coercive-field comparisons depend on whether the reported quantity is pristine, wake-up-conditioned, hysteresis-derived, or PUND-corrected \cite{song2021ferro,kim2020tin,kashir2021wake}. In the TiN/HZO/TiN chamber study, pristine $P_r$ changes across both deposition and anneal temperatures, and deposition at $120\,^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ gives significantly lower $P_r$ than at 200 or $250\,^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$; anneal comparisons therefore require a matched chamber temperature \cite{park2022chamber}. Thickness adds a separate constraint because ALD HZO shows increasing $P_r$ and decreasing $E_c$ as film thickness increases \cite{kim2014inductive}. For $(20,20)\times3$ nanolaminates annealed at $550\,^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$, unseeded and top-seeded specimens form one similar P--E pair, whereas bottom-seeded and dual-seeded specimens form another; the $650\,^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ anneal degrades ferroelectric properties through monoclinic-phase formation, and greater nanolaminate thickness trades higher remanent polarization against poorer durability \cite{park2023seed}. For W/HZO/W processed with a 15 s ozone pulse and a $700\,^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$/5 s anneal, pristine and woken values remain distinct \cite{kashir2021wake}. Under the $700\,^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$/5 s anneal, PUND-corrected $2P_r$ differs between W/HZO/W and W/Pt/HZO/W \cite{kashir2021wake}. In sub-6 nm HZO/Zr-RL/HZO at 1.0 V, the $2P_r$/coercive-voltage pair belongs to the same operating condition \cite{liu2025zrrich}. Separately, the 25--$125\,^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ temperature series reports stable $2P_r$ without a significant coercive-voltage shift for that stack \cite{liu2025zrrich}.
\subsection{Leakage-Current Constraints}

Leakage comparisons require matched bias, conditioning, and stack \cite{park2022chamber,kaatranen2026unif}. For TiN/HZO/TiN, leakage measured at $-3$ V rises with anneal temperature at fixed chamber temperature and with chamber temperature at fixed anneal temperature \cite{park2022chamber}. The SS/SL comparison instead reports maxima after wake-up at $\pm3.0$ V, so those results do not define a common limit with the chamber/anneal series \cite{kaatranen2026unif}. The current reported at 12 V for a $1.1\times10^{-3}$ cm$^2$ electrode is identified as domain-switching current, not leakage current \cite{kim2014inductive}.
\subsection{Defect Density, Endurance, and Reliability}

Reliability outcomes remain specific to process history, stack, and electrical stress rather than defining a transferable cycle-count window \cite{park2022chamber,choi2025purge,kaatranen2026unif,liu2025zrrich}. In purge-series HZO capacitors, a 3 s precursor purge shows no fatigue or breakdown through $10^9$ cycles, whereas the 30 and 90 s variants break at $2\times10^8$ and $6\times10^7$ cycles, respectively \cite{choi2025purge}. The chamber-temperature series presents a different tradeoff: all tested chamber/anneal combinations avoid breakdown through $10^8$ cycles, yet fatigue begins earlier as chamber temperature rises; the accompanying oxygen-vacancy increase is linked to higher $P_r$ and a lower critical cycle count \cite{park2022chamber}. In SS/SL wafers, increasing cycling voltage shortens breakdown life for both architectures and SL outlasts SS at matched voltage; after $10^6$ cycles, post-wake-up retention loss remains below 4\% through $10^4$ s \cite{kaatranen2026unif}. At a saturated frequency of 100 kHz, endurance decreases by one decade for each 0.22 V increase \cite{liu2025zrrich}. For the HZO/Zr-RL/HZO stack, endurance reaches up to $10^{11}$ cycles at cycling voltages no greater than 1.5 V; the approximately $10^{11}$-cycle result at 1.0 V is explicitly breakdown-limited \cite{liu2025zrrich}. The 10-year retention result for that stack belongs to 1.0 V and $125\,^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ \cite{liu2025zrrich}. Reported FeFET, MFM-capacitor, and FeRAM-array endurance values remain configuration-specific because their stacks, fields, frequencies, and operating temperatures differ \cite{liao2023postmoore}.

\section{Electrical Qualification and Metrology}
\subsection{Crystalline-Phase and Structural Characterization}

GI-XRD and local imaging are paired because GI-XRD confirms the orthorhombic phase structurally, whereas STEM directly confirms its presence and can follow an interfacial monoclinic-to-orthorhombic transition during wake-up cycling \cite{song2021ferro}. An orthorhombic (111) reflection at $2\theta=30.4^\circ$ supports the diffraction assignment, but three metastable phases become indistinguishable when similar $d$-spacings, fine-crystallite peak broadening, and uncertain biaxial strain coincide \cite{song2021ferro,choi2025purge}. Instrumental resolution also limits an inference from an undetected monoclinic peak; absence in the scan therefore does not establish phase exclusion \cite{kashir2021wake}. Two-dimensional XRD supplies a processed-film stress estimate through $\sin^2\psi$ analysis, while FTIR--ATR supplies an independent absorption spectrum; the reported sharp feature at 670~cm$^{-1}$ is an artifact and cannot support structural interpretation \cite{choi2025purge}. STEM additionally establishes film thickness and continuity, but HfO$_2$/ZrO$_2$ sublayers in a 5.5-nm superlattice lamella could not be distinguished when bottom-TiN roughness approached the film scale \cite{song2021ferro,kaatranen2026unif}. Structural comparisons must therefore retain diffraction geometry, instrumental resolution, and cross-sectional specimen condition rather than equating every phase-sensitive output \cite{choi2025purge,kim2020tin,kaatranen2026unif}.
\subsection{Film-Thickness and Uniformity Measurement}

Spectroscopic ellipsometry and cross-sectional TEM provide a thickness cross-check: ellipsometry measures film and seed-layer thickness, while TEM confirms the ferroelectric-layer thickness and tests whether it changes across chamber-temperature conditions \cite{park2022chamber,park2023seed}. Multipoint ellipsometry extends the measurement to spatial uniformity by sampling 49 wafer positions with a 3-mm edge exclusion \cite{neuber2022peald}. Its interpretation remains reactor-specific: the standard HSO process shows an inlet-to-outlet thickness decrease in a crossflow reactor, whereas the hybrid process shows an edge-enhanced ring in a showerhead reactor \cite{neuber2022peald}. The similar reported thickness standard deviations therefore do not imply identical spatial behavior \cite{neuber2022peald}. In the wafer-scale SS/SL study, thickness comes from silicon reference pieces grown with the characterized wafers, so those values are not direct cross-sections of every capacitor site \cite{kaatranen2026unif}. Comparisons consequently preserve the sampling pattern, edge exclusion, reactor geometry, and reference-piece versus device-stack distinction \cite{neuber2022peald,kaatranen2026unif}.
\subsection{Interface and Defect Characterization}

Chemical profiling and cross-sectional imaging are paired to combine composition and depth profiles from spectroscopy or SIMS with interface morphology from electron microscopy \cite{park2022chamber,kim2020tin,liu2025zrrich,kashir2021wake}. O~1s XPS separates lattice-oxygen and sub-oxide components, while EDS supplies Hf, Zr, and O atomic fractions for chamber-temperature variants \cite{park2022chamber}. D-SIMS provides an oxygen depth profile and confirms bottom-TiN oxidation during deposition; ToF-SIMS tracks carbon and nitrogen impurities remaining after shortened purges \cite{kim2020tin,choi2025purge}. HRTEM then distinguishes interfacial regions at W/HZO from the sharp Pt/HZO interface, and HR-STEM supplies atomic-scale imaging of prepared lamellae \cite{liu2025zrrich,kashir2021wake}. These outputs are complementary rather than interchangeable: laboratory XPS reaches only about 5~nm and precludes a realistic top electrode, whereas HAXPES increases depth sensitivity by one to two orders of magnitude for nondestructive vacancy profiling through electrodes \cite{schroeder2023road}. Quantification also retains method-specific limits, because adventitious carbon and absorbed OH confound XPS-derived oxygen stoichiometry and ToF-SIMS does not quantify hydrogen ions \cite{choi2025purge}. Interface comparisons should therefore couple morphology with chemical depth information and stop short of treating either signal alone as a complete defect description \cite{schroeder2023road,choi2025purge,kashir2021wake}.
\subsection{Electrical Stressing and Qualification Protocols}

Qualification uses a defined conditioning block followed by a specified readout, not a cycle count alone \cite{choi2025purge,liu2025zrrich,kaatranen2026unif}. One protocol applies $5\times10^3$ square-wave wake-up cycles before and after measurement, while another applies $10^3$ square-wave cycles before PUND; their fields or amplitudes and frequencies differ \cite{choi2025purge,liu2025zrrich}. Endurance protocols likewise range from 3-V trapezoids with 1-$\mu$s ramps and holds to 2.0-MV/cm bipolar pulses at 10~kHz with 2-$\mu$s width and 20-ns edges \cite{park2022chamber,choi2025purge}. Electrode referencing is also reversed between reported setups: one biases the TiN top electrode with the bottom grounded, whereas another biases the bottom electrode with the top grounded \cite{kim2020tin,liu2025zrrich}. Qualification comparisons must therefore preserve conditioning state, waveform shape, amplitude or field, frequency, pulse timing, cycle count, and driven electrode \cite{park2022chamber,choi2025purge,kim2020tin,liu2025zrrich}.
\subsection{Leakage and Breakdown Measurement}

Leakage and breakdown tests separate sweep-based failure, spatial leakage mapping, and time-zero dielectric breakdown \cite{park2023seed,kaatranen2026unif,liu2025zrrich}. The seed-layer comparison uses a 0--15-V DC sweep in 0.5-V steps, whereas wafer mapping samples leakage at $\pm3.0$~V against radial bottom-TiN thickness \cite{park2023seed,kaatranen2026unif}. Excessive crystallization is associated with a leakage path and reduced breakdown in the anneal series, while temperatures above 720~$^\circ$C produce large leakage and short circuits in W/HZO/W capacitors \cite{park2023seed,kashir2021wake}. The reported TZDB comparison specifies stack and anneal variation but no waveform, electrode area, or stress time, so interpretation stops at time-zero comparison across those conditions \cite{liu2025zrrich}.
\subsection{Hysteresis, Cycling, and Reliability Testing}

Hysteresis and pulsed measurements are paired to distinguish a loop response from switched polarization \cite{song2021ferro,schroeder2023road}. PUND is used when leakage exceeds polarization-switching current and removes leakage and dielectric contributions from the switched-polarization result, whereas P--E or P--V loops record hysteresis under their stated waveform and frequency \cite{song2021ferro,schroeder2023road,kim2020tin,neuber2022peald}. Designed pulse trains add a switching-dynamics measurement that is distinct from loop or endurance acquisition \cite{liu2025zrrich}. Reliability evaluation then couples cycling blocks to subsequent or interleaved readout: one seed-layer study applies rectangular cycling before triangular-pulse measurement, while the wafer-scale study uses 30-$\mu$s up/down pulses with 5-$\mu$s breaks and 2--3-ms triangular PUND sweeps during or after wake-up \cite{park2023seed,kaatranen2026unif}. Wake-up and fatigue must be resolved along the cycle trajectory, because bottom- and dual-seeded devices show wake-up before fatigue after the polarization maximum, whereas unseeded and top-seeded devices fatigue without a wake-up effect \cite{park2023seed}. Retention and high-field switching also require their full setup: the reported retention test uses 12-V, 600-ns pulses on a $1.1\times10^{-3}$-cm$^2$ electrode, for which severe leakage is reported, and verification below 5~nm is described as leakage dominated \cite{kim2014inductive,mikolajick2018mrs}. Protocols differing in waveform, field, frequency, pulse width, electrode area, conditioning state, or readout method therefore support within-protocol trends, not unqualified cycle-to-cycle equivalence \cite{park2023seed,kim2014inductive,kaatranen2026unif,liu2025zrrich}.
